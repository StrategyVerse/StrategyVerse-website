The Gap

The problems we help you solve

Most communications problems are not a lack of effort — they are a lack of direction.

  • Reactive, not proactive. You respond to whatever comes up instead of driving a plan of your own.
  • Inconsistent messaging. Different teams and channels tell slightly different versions of your story.
  • Activity without direction. Plenty of PR and content, but little clear link to business goals.
  • A story that does not land. What you say is accurate, but it does not make people care.
  • Communications as an afterthought. Treated as a tactic to switch on, not a lever to plan around.
  • No way to measure it. You cannot tell what is working, or show the value of what you do.

FAQ

Communications strategy, answered

What is a communications strategy?

A communications strategy is the plan that decides what your organisation says, to whom, through which channels, and why — all aligned to your business goals. It defines your positioning (what you stand for and how you are different), your core narrative and key messages, the audiences and stakeholders that matter, the channels you will use to reach them, and how you will measure whether it is working. It is the layer above individual PR campaigns, content, and social posts that keeps them all pulling in the same direction.

Why does my company need a communications strategy?

Without a strategy, communications becomes reactive — a press release here, a few posts there, each disconnected from the next and from the business itself. The result is effort without momentum. A communications strategy ties everything you say back to where the business is going, so your messaging is consistent across teams and channels, your spend is focused, and your audiences come away with a clear and repeated understanding of who you are. It also gives you a way to measure communications against goals rather than vanity metrics.

What is the difference between PR and communications strategy?

PR — media relations, coverage, press materials — is one channel through which a strategy is expressed. A communications strategy is broader: it sets the positioning and messaging that PR, content, social media, internal communications, and executive visibility all draw from. Think of the strategy as the blueprint and PR as one of the ways you build from it. Strong PR without a strategy produces scattered coverage; a strategy without execution produces a document nobody uses. You need both working together.

What does a communications strategy engagement include?

A typical engagement with Strategyverse begins with a communications audit and landscape review, then moves to positioning and a messaging architecture, a core narrative and key messages, audience and stakeholder mapping, a channel strategy across earned, owned, and social media, a campaign roadmap, and a measurement framework with clear KPIs. Depending on your needs it can also cover internal communications and spokesperson alignment. The output is a strategy built to be used day to day, not a deck that sits in a drawer.
