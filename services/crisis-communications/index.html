The Stakes

The problems we help you solve

Most reputational damage in a crisis is self-inflicted — caused not by the event itself, but by how the organisation responds to it.

◆
  • Silence that reads as guilt. Going quiet while a story runs lets others write your narrative for you.
  • Getting the facts wrong in public. Rushing out a statement before you understand what happened almost always makes things worse.
  • Mixed messages. Different people saying different things to media, staff, and customers erodes trust fast.
  • Media enquiries no one is handling. Journalists on deadline will run the story with or without you.
  • Forgetting your own people. Employees, partners, and customers hear the news too — and how you treat them shows.
  • No plan for afterwards. The crisis ends, but the reputation work has only just begun.

FAQ

Crisis communications, answered

What is crisis communication consulting?

Crisis communication consulting is specialist counsel that helps an organisation decide what to say, to whom, and when during an event that threatens its reputation — a product failure, a leadership controversy, a data breach, a regulatory action, or a story spreading on social media. A consultant helps you assess the situation quickly, agree a position, prepare holding statements and spokespeople, manage media and stakeholder enquiries, and protect the brand through to recovery. Strategyverse provides this as both an on-call service during a live crisis and a preparedness programme before one happens.

How quickly can Strategyverse respond to a crisis?

A crisis does not keep office hours, and neither does crisis counsel. Our team is set up to engage at short notice — including outside standard office hours — to help you take control of the first few hours, which usually decide how the story unfolds. The fastest responses come from clients who already have a crisis plan and an agreed point of contact in place, which is why we recommend building a crisis communication plan before anything goes wrong.

What should a company do first in a PR crisis?

Before issuing any public statement, establish the facts internally and agree who speaks for the organisation. The most damaging mistakes are made in the first hour — guessing at facts, going silent while the story runs, or letting multiple people comment without coordination. Acknowledge the issue, say what you are doing about it, and commit to updating people as you learn more. A prepared holding statement and a single, briefed spokesperson make this far easier.

Do you only help during a crisis, or also prepare beforehand?

Both. Many clients first come to us mid-crisis, but the work we are most proud of is the crisis that never becomes public damage because the organisation was ready. Our preparedness work includes risk and scenario mapping, a crisis communication plan, pre-drafted statement frameworks, spokesperson and media training, and clear escalation protocols so your team knows exactly who does what when something happens.
