FAQ

Executive personal branding, answered

What is executive personal branding?

Executive personal branding is the deliberate work of shaping how a leader is known publicly — what they stand for, the expertise they are recognised for, and the way they show up in the media, on LinkedIn, on stage, and in their industry. For a CXO (any C-suite leader, such as a CEO, CFO, CMO, or CTO) or a founder, a strong personal brand turns individual credibility into a business asset: it attracts talent, investors, customers, and media attention. It is not vanity or self-promotion; it is positioning a real person's genuine expertise so the right audiences see it consistently.

Why do CEOs and CXOs need a personal brand?

People trust people more than they trust logos. When a CEO or CXO is visible and credible, that trust transfers to the company — buyers shortlist it, journalists call for comment, strong candidates apply, and investors take the first meeting. A leader with no public presence leaves that ground to competitors who are willing to be seen. In regulated or considered-purchase sectors especially, the personal credibility of the leadership is often the deciding factor, which is why executive branding has become a core part of corporate communications rather than an optional extra.

What does an executive personal branding engagement include?

A typical engagement with Strategyverse starts with positioning — defining what the leader should be known for and who needs to hear it. From there it covers a messaging narrative and point of view, a content plan (bylines, posts, commentary, and talks), an active LinkedIn presence written in the leader's own voice, media and speaking opportunities, and alignment between the executive's personal brand and the company's brand. The balance shifts depending on the leader's goals, time, and starting point.

How is this different from just posting on LinkedIn?

Posting is a tactic; personal branding is a strategy. Regular LinkedIn activity only works when it flows from a clear position — a defined point of view, consistent themes, and a reason for the right people to pay attention. We start with that strategy, then use LinkedIn, earned media, and speaking as channels to express it. The result is a coherent reputation rather than a stream of disconnected posts, and it extends well beyond LinkedIn into how the leader is known across their whole industry.
